What is BMI?

Body Mass Index, or BMI, is a value calculated from weight and height. It is commonly used as a screening measure for weight status.

  • 📐 BMI uses height and weight to produce a numerical value.
  • 📊 Adult BMI is divided into broad weight categories.
  • 💪 BMI does not directly measure body fat or muscle mass.
  • ❤️ BMI is a screening measure, not a diagnosis.
  • 👶 Ages 2–19 require age- and sex-specific BMI interpretation.

Understanding BMI

Learn how BMI is calculated, what adult categories mean, and why BMI has limitations.

📐 BMI Formula

BMI = Weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared.

📊 Screening Tool

BMI is commonly used as a screening measure and is not a diagnosis by itself.

👶 Children & Teens

Ages 2–19 require BMI-for-age interpretation based on age and sex.

Example: A person weighing 70 kg and measuring 1.75 meters tall has a BMI of approximately 22.9.

BMI for Children and Teenagers

Adult BMI categories should not be used for children and teenagers. For ages 2–19, BMI is interpreted according to age and sex because body composition changes during growth.

  • Below the 5th percentile: Underweight
  • 5th percentile to below the 85th percentile: Healthy weight
  • 85th percentile to below the 95th percentile: Overweight
  • 95th percentile or higher: Obesity

Limitations of BMI

BMI does not distinguish between muscle, bone, water and fat. Two people can therefore have the same BMI while having substantially different body compositions.

  • BMI does not directly measure body-fat percentage.
  • BMI does not show where body fat is distributed.
  • BMI does not directly measure muscle mass.
  • BMI does not capture every factor affecting health.

Important: BMI is a screening measure and should not be used by itself to diagnose a medical condition.

Using BMI Responsibly

BMI can provide a useful starting point for understanding weight relative to height, but a broader health assessment may consider additional information.

  • Waist circumference
  • Blood pressure
  • Physical activity
  • Dietary patterns
  • Medical history
  • Family history
  • Other clinical measurements

BMI During Pregnancy

BMI is not interpreted in the same way during pregnancy. Pregnancy involves expected changes in body weight and composition, so pregnancy-specific medical guidance should be used instead.

BMI and Muscular People

People with substantial muscle mass can have a relatively high BMI because muscle contributes to body weight. BMI may therefore not accurately reflect body-fat levels in some highly muscular individuals.
